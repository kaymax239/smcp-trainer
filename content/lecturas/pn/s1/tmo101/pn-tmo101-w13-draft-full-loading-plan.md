Before loading, the chief officer prepares a {{loading plan}}. The plan shows where each cargo goes and in what order. A good plan keeps the ship safe, strong and within its limits. The cadet must understand the main ideas behind the plan.

The first idea is {{stowage}}. Stowage is the way we place cargo in the {{hold}}. Heavy cargo usually goes low in the hold. Light cargo goes on top. Cargo for the first port of discharge should be easy to reach. Some cargoes cannot go together, for example food and some chemicals. Keeping these cargoes apart is called {{segregation}}.

The second idea is the loading equipment. Cargo comes on board with the ship's {{crane}} or with port cranes. Each crane has a safe working load. The crew must not lift more than this load. A {{hatch cover}} closes each hold. After loading, the crew closes the hatch covers. Then they check that the covers are watertight.

The third idea is the distribution of weight along the ship. The water supports the hull along its length. But the cargo weight is not always equal along the ship. Too much weight at the ends makes the middle of the ship bend up. This is {{hogging}}. Too much weight in the middle makes the middle bend down. This is {{sagging}}. Both conditions put stress on the hull. A good plan spreads the weight to avoid them.

The fourth idea is stability. The {{centre of gravity}} of the ship depends on the position of the weights. Heavy cargo high in the ship raises the centre of gravity. A high centre of gravity makes the ship less stable. Heavy cargo low in the ship keeps it more stable. Tanks that are only partly full also reduce stability.

The last idea is the {{load line}}. Every ton of cargo increases the {{displacement}} of the ship. The draft increases too. The officer must know the maximum draft for the voyage. This depends on the seasonal zone and the season. At the end of loading, the ship must not be deeper than the correct mark.

During loading, the officer reads the drafts and checks the plan often. The plan is not fixed. If there is a problem, the officer stops the work and corrects the plan. A good loading plan brings all these ideas together. Stowage, equipment, hull stress, stability and the load line all matter.
