A ship needs weight low down to stay safe at sea. With little cargo, the ship is light and high in the water. The propeller and the rudder may not be deep enough. The ship can also be less stable. For this reason, ships carry sea water as {{ballast}}.

Ballast water goes into special tanks. A {{ballast tank}} is a steel tank in the hull. Many ballast tanks are in the {{double bottom}}. This is the space between the outer bottom and the inner bottom. There are also tanks at the ends of the ship. The {{fore peak tank}} is at the bow. The {{aft peak tank}} is at the stern. Ships also have tanks for fresh water and for fuel.

Each tank has a {{sounding pipe}}. The crew uses it to measure the level of liquid in the tank. A sounding tape goes down the pipe to the bottom of the tank. Many ships also have remote gauges. Each tank also has an {{air pipe}}. When the tank fills, air goes out through this pipe. When the tank empties, air comes in. Without air pipes, the pressure can damage a tank.

Ballast changes the {{draft}} of the ship. The draft is the depth of the hull under the water. The crew reads it on the {{draft marks}} at the bow, midships and stern. When we add ballast, the ship goes deeper. The draft increases. When we remove ballast, the draft decreases.

Ballast also changes the {{trim}}. Trim is the difference between the forward draft and the aft draft. When the aft draft is greater, the ship trims by the stern. When the forward draft is greater, it trims by the head. When both drafts are the same, the ship is on an {{even keel}}. Weight at one end makes that end go down. Less weight at one end makes that end come up.

Ballast also affects {{stability}}. Stability is the ability of the ship to return upright after it heels. Weight low in the ship, for example in the double bottom, helps stability. A tank that is only partly full lets the water move from side to side. This moving water reduces stability.

The deck officer plans the ballast work. On many ships, the engine room operates the ballast pumps. Clear communication between deck and engine room is very important. The officer gives the tank, the action and the reason. After the operation, the officer reads the drafts again.

Ballast water from one port can carry small sea organisms to another port. For this reason, IMO has special rules for the management of ballast water.
