In simple terms, a ship is a large vessel for navigation at sea. It floats, it moves with its own propulsion, and it carries cargo or people. Some ships also give a service to other ships or to ports. Every officer must know the main types of ships and the names of their parts.

Merchant ships have different types, and each type carries a different cargo. A general cargo ship carries goods in boxes, bags, drums or single pieces. A {{bulk carrier}} carries dry cargo without packing, such as grain, coal or iron ore. A {{tanker}} carries liquids in tanks, for example crude oil, fuel or chemicals. A {{container ship}} carries standard steel boxes. A ro-ro ship carries vehicles that roll on and roll off. Passenger ships and ferries carry people. Tugs and supply vessels give services to other ships and to platforms. In the port of Tampico, you can see several of these types.

Each part of a ship has a name. The main body of the ship is the {{hull}}. The front part is the {{bow}}, and the back part is the {{stern}}. The middle part is called amidships. Stand on board and look forward to the bow. The left side is {{port}}, and the right side is {{starboard}}. The decks are the floors of the ship. The main deck closes the top of the hull from bow to stern. The keel is the lowest part of the hull, along the centre line.

Every ship also has principal dimensions. {{Length overall}} is the distance from the front end of the bow to the back end of the stern. The {{beam}} is the width of the ship at its widest part. The depth is the vertical distance from the keel to the main deck. The {{draft}} is the vertical distance from the keel to the waterline. The {{freeboard}} is the distance from the waterline up to the main deck. When the ship loads cargo, it goes deeper in the water. Then the draft gets bigger, and the freeboard gets smaller.

These names and dimensions are important for deck work. Port authorities, pilots and officers use them every day. They use them to plan the berth, the channel and the cargo work. They also use them in radio messages and in reports. In your task, you must choose three types of ships. Then you must explain why each dimension matters on deck.
