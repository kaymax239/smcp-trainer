Cargo must stay in its place during the voyage. Good stowage and strong lashings hold it in position. In a heavy sea, the ship can {{roll}} strongly from side to side. If a {{lashing}} breaks, or the stowage is poor, the cargo can move. This is a {{cargo shift}}. It is one of the most dangerous situations at sea.

To understand the danger, remember the {{centre of gravity}}. It is the point where all the weight of the ship acts. When the ship is {{upright}}, the centre of gravity is on the centre line. Waves or wind can make the ship {{heel}} for a short time. A stable ship comes back upright after it heels. This ability is called {{stability}}.

When cargo moves to one side, its weight moves with it. The centre of gravity moves to that side too. Now the ship does not come back upright. It stays inclined to one side. This permanent inclination is a {{list}}. A list is different from a heel. A heel is temporary, but a list stays.

A list is dangerous for several reasons. The deck edge on the low side is closer to the water. Waves can come on deck more easily. Water can enter through open doors or ventilators. The ship rolls more to the low side. More cargo can move, and the list can become bigger.

Liquids can also reduce stability. In a tank that is only partly full, the liquid moves when the ship heels. This is the {{free surface}} effect. The moving liquid acts like a higher centre of gravity. Water on deck or in a hold has the same effect. For this reason, the officer must always know which tanks are partly full.

Good cargo securing prevents most cargo shifts. The crew uses lashings, wires, chains and wood. Wood between the cargo pieces is called {{dunnage}}. It stops the cargo moving and protects it. Before the voyage and in bad weather, the crew checks all the securing. Hatch covers and doors must also be closed, so water cannot enter.

When a cargo shift happens, the officer must act fast but calmly. First, the officer understands the situation. How big is the list? Is it getting bigger? Is water coming in? Then the officer reports to the master at once. Clear and correct information helps the master decide.
