A ship is a long steel structure. Along its length, two forces act on it all the time. Weight pulls the ship down. {{Buoyancy}} pushes the ship up. The weight comes from the steel, the cargo, the fuel and the ballast. The buoyancy comes from the water under the hull. At each part of the ship, weight and buoyancy are often not equal. Then the hull bends a little. This bending creates {{stress}} in the structure.

There are two main types of longitudinal {{bending}}. The first type is {{hogging}}. Its Spanish name is quebranto. In hogging, the middle of the ship goes up, and the bow and the stern go down. The deck is in tension, and the bottom is in compression. The second type is {{sagging}}. Its Spanish name is arrufo. In sagging, the middle of the ship goes down, and the ends go up. Now the deck is in compression, and the bottom is in tension.

In {{still water}}, the main cause is the distribution of weight. Imagine a lot of weight at the bow and the stern, and little weight {{amidships}}. The ends go down, and the ship hogs. Now imagine a lot of weight in the middle holds, and little weight at the ends. The middle goes down, and the ship sags.

Waves also cause bending. Sometimes a {{wave crest}} is under the middle of the ship. The middle has more support from the water. The ends have less support, so the ship hogs. At other times, the crests are at the bow and the stern. Then there is a {{wave trough}} amidships. The middle has less support, so the ship sags. In bad weather, the ship changes between hogging and sagging many times. This repeated bending can damage the hull over time.

Every ship has a limit. The shipyard builds it with a certain {{longitudinal strength}}. Officers must keep the stresses inside the safe limits. Before loading, the chief officer plans the cargo and the ballast. A {{loading computer}} calculates the bending moments and shear forces for each condition. The officer compares the results with the limits of the ship. The crew also reads the drafts at the bow, at the stern and amidships. A comparison of these drafts shows hogging or sagging. Some large ships also have a hull stress monitoring system with sensors.

Very high stresses can cause cracks in the deck or the bottom. In extreme cases, they can break the ship. Good loading plans and careful monitoring prevent this danger.
