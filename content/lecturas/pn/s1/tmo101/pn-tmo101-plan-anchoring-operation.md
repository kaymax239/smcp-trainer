When a ship waits for a berth, it often anchors outside the port. The anchoring equipment is at the bow, on the {{forecastle}}. The forecastle is the raised deck at the front of the ship. A team of officers and seamen works there during the manoeuvre.

The {{anchor}} is a heavy steel piece. It holds the ship to the sea bottom. Most merchant ships have two anchors, one on each side of the bow. When the ship is at sea, each anchor sits in its {{hawse pipe}}. This is a strong steel tube from the deck to the side of the bow.

The anchor is connected to the {{cable}}. On merchant ships, the cable is a heavy steel chain. It comes in standard lengths. Each length is called a {{shackle}}. Sailors count the cable in shackles. When the anchor is up, the cable stays in the {{chain locker}}. The chain locker is a space under the forecastle.

The {{windlass}} is the machine that lifts and lowers the cable. It can heave the anchor up from the bottom. It can also walk the anchor out slowly. The windlass has a {{brake}}. The crew uses the brake to control the cable. With the brake, they can stop the cable or let it run. The {{chain stopper}} is a strong device on deck, between the windlass and the hawse pipe. It holds the cable when the ship is at anchor. In this way, the windlass does not take all the load.

The anchoring manoeuvre also needs the bridge. The bridge controls the speed with the {{propeller}}. It controls the heading with the {{rudder}}. The ship comes to the anchor position slowly. The ship must lose almost all its speed before the anchor goes down. A ship that moves too fast can damage the cable and the windlass.

The officer must decide how much cable to pay out. The depth of water is a key factor. The weather and the type of sea bottom are also important. Too little cable can let the anchor drag. The officer studies the chart and the conditions before stations.

Good communication between the bridge and the forecastle is essential. The forecastle officer reports the direction of the cable. The officer also reports how many shackles are out. The bridge gives clear orders, and the forecastle repeats them. Short and standard phrases avoid mistakes. A good team works calmly and safely.
