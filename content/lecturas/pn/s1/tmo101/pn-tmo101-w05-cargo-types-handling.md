Ships carry many kinds of cargo. Each kind needs a different type of handling. Before loading, the officer must know the type of each cargo. Then the crew can choose the right equipment, the right place and the right care.

{{General cargo}} is cargo in separate units. Examples are boxes, bags, drums, machines and steel pieces. Often the cargo comes on a {{pallet}}. The {{stevedore}} gangs lift it with a {{sling}} and a crane. General cargo needs careful stowage. It must not move, fall or break during the voyage.

{{Dry bulk}} is loose dry cargo without packing. Examples are grain, coal, iron ore, cement and fertilizer. It goes directly into the hold with conveyors or grabs. The holds must be clean and dry before loading. Some bulk cargoes make a lot of dust. Others can shift in the hold when the ship rolls.

{{Liquid bulk}} is cargo like crude oil, fuel, chemicals or vegetable oil. Tankers carry it in their tanks. The cargo moves through pipes and pumps, not with cranes. The crew must prevent leaks, spills and fire. They watch the tank levels and the pressure in the lines all the time.

A {{container}} is a standard steel box. It protects the cargo inside. Containers move fast with special cranes at the terminal. Each container has its own number. The officer checks the number and the position of each container on board.

{{Reefer}} cargo needs a low temperature. Examples are fruit, meat, fish and some medicines. Many reefer containers have their own cooling unit. The crew connects them to the electric power of the ship. During the voyage, the crew checks the temperature of each reefer container.

{{Dangerous goods}} can explode, burn or poison people. Some can also pollute the sea. At sea, these goods follow the {{IMDG Code}} of the IMO. Each package must have a {{label}} with its class. The crew must keep some dangerous goods away from other cargoes. This separation is called segregation. Dangerous goods always need special attention from the officer.

On a multipurpose ship, several types of cargo can travel together. The officer must read the loading list carefully. Each cargo type has its own needs on deck and in the hold.
