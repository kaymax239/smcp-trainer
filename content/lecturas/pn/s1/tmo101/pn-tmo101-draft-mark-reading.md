The {{draft}} of a ship is the vertical distance from the bottom of the {{keel}} to the {{waterline}}. The draft changes when the ship loads or discharges cargo, ballast or fuel. For this reason, officers read the draft many times in port.

To read the draft, the ship has {{draft marks}} on the hull. They are numbers painted or welded on the plating. There are draft marks at the bow, at the stern and {{amidships}}. They are on both sides of the ship. So the officer can read six drafts: forward, midships and aft, on port and on starboard.

Most ships use metric draft marks. Each number is 10 centimetres high. The bottom edge of a number marks the exact draft of that number. The officer looks at the point where the water touches the marks. With waves, the water goes up and down. So the officer watches for some time and takes a middle value. Reading from the quay or from a small boat is often easier and more accurate. The officer writes down each draft with its place on the ship.

The {{freeboard}} is the vertical distance from the waterline up to the {{deck line}}. It is measured at the side of the ship, amidships. The deck line is a horizontal line painted at the level of the freeboard deck. Freeboard gives the ship a safety reserve. It keeps the deck high above the sea and gives reserve buoyancy.

A ship must not load too much cargo. For this reason, every merchant ship has load line marks amidships on both sides. The circle with a horizontal line through it is the {{Plimsoll mark}}. It takes its name from Samuel Plimsoll, a British politician. The line through the circle is at the level of the {{summer load line}}. Next to the circle, there are more lines for different seasons and waters. They have letters: TF, F, T, S, W and WNA. Each {{load line}} shows the maximum draft for that zone or season.

In {{fresh water}}, the ship floats deeper than in sea water. For this reason, the fresh water lines are higher. In winter, the sea is often more dangerous. For this reason, the winter line is lower, and the ship carries less cargo. The International Convention on Load Lines gives the rules for these marks. During your observation at the quay, look for the draft marks, the deck line and the load line marks.
