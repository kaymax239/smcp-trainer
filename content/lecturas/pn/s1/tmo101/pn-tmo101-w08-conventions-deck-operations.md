The {{IMO}} is the International Maritime Organization. It is part of the United Nations. Its main job is to make shipping safe, secure and clean. The IMO writes international rules for ships. A big set of these rules is called a {{convention}}. Many countries sign a convention. Then their ships must follow it.

Three conventions are very important for a deck officer. They are SOLAS, MARPOL and STCW. Each one has a different purpose. On deck, you can see all three in your daily work.

{{SOLAS}} means Safety of Life at Sea. It is about the safety of the ship and the people on board. It covers the construction of the ship and fire protection. It also covers {{life-saving equipment}}, radio communication and safe navigation. On deck, the crew checks lifeboats, life jackets and fire equipment. These checks protect life at sea.

{{MARPOL}} is the convention for the prevention of {{pollution}} from ships. It covers pollution by oil, by chemicals and by garbage. It also covers sewage and air pollution from ships. On deck, the crew sorts the garbage and follows strict rules before any discharge. The crew also keeps oil away from the sea. The ship writes oil and garbage operations in a {{record book}}.

{{STCW}} means Standards of Training, Certification and Watchkeeping for Seafarers. It is about the people, not the ship. It says what training and certificates seafarers need. It also gives rules for {{watchkeeping}}. The officer on watch must be trained, rested and alert. On deck, the watch keeps a good {{lookout}} at all times. The officer also knows the ship's equipment and the safety procedures.

In a real operation, the three conventions work together. SOLAS keeps the ship and the crew safe. MARPOL protects the sea. STCW makes sure the people are ready for the job. For example, cargo work and departure are busy times on deck. The deck team thinks about safety, pollution and the watch at the same time. A good deck officer remembers all three conventions before every operation.
