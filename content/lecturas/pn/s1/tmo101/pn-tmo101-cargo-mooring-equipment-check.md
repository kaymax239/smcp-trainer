On a general cargo ship, the deck has two main groups of equipment. The first group is for cargo work. The second group is for mooring and anchoring. A deck officer must know the name and the purpose of each item.

Cargo goes into the {{hold}}, a large space under the deck. Each hold has an opening on the deck, called a hatch. A {{hatch cover}} closes this opening. Modern hatch covers are strong steel panels. Many of them open and close with a hydraulic system. When they are closed, they must be watertight. Rubber packing and cleats keep the water out of the hold. The crew keeps the packing and the hatch coamings clean. Sea water in a hold can damage the cargo and put the ship in danger.

To lift cargo, many ships have their own gear. A {{crane}} lifts cargo with a wire and a hook. The operator sits in a cabin and turns the crane over the hold or over the quay. Older ships often use a {{derrick}}. A derrick is a long boom with wires, blocks and winches. A derrick needs a strong support. A {{mast}} or a {{king post}} gives this support. Their base connects strongly to the deck structure, because they carry heavy loads.

Mooring equipment keeps the ship alongside the quay. Most of this equipment is on the {{forecastle}} at the bow and on the poop deck at the stern. Each {{mooring line}} goes from the ship to the quay. On the ship, the line passes through a {{fairlead}} or a chock at the side. The crew can make the line fast on a {{bollard}}. A {{mooring winch}} pulls the line in and holds it. Each line has a name for its position. There are head lines, breast lines, springs and stern lines.

The forecastle also has the anchoring equipment. The main machine there is the {{windlass}}. It lifts and lowers the anchor and the anchor chain. On many ships, the windlass also has drums for mooring lines. So the same area serves for mooring and for anchoring.

Cargo gear and mooring gear work under very high loads. A wire, a line or a hatch cover can fail without warning. People near the equipment can suffer serious injuries. For this reason, the crew checks the equipment before every operation. The officer also controls who stands near the equipment during the work.
