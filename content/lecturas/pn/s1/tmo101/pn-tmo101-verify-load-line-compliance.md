A ship must not carry too much weight. An {{overloaded}} ship sits too deep in the water. Waves come on deck easily, and the ship is in danger. For this reason, every merchant ship has a {{load line}}. The load line shows the deepest safe draft for the ship. There is an international convention on load lines, and it is an IMO convention.

{{Freeboard}} is the vertical distance from the waterline to the deck. It is measured at midships, up to the top of the {{deck line}}. The deck line is a mark on the side of the hull. More cargo means a deeper {{draft}} and less freeboard. Less cargo means more freeboard.

The load line marks are on both sides of the ship, at midships. The main mark is the {{Plimsoll mark}}. It is a ring with a horizontal line through its centre. This line is at the level of the {{summer load line}}. Two letters next to the ring show the organization that assigned the load line.

Next to the ring there is a group of lines. Each line has letters. S is Summer. W is Winter. T is Tropical. F is Fresh, for fresh water. TF is Tropical Fresh. Some ships also have a WNA line, for Winter North Atlantic. The upper edge of each line shows the maximum draft for those conditions.

Why are there different lines? The sea is different in different places and seasons. In winter, the weather is often worse. The ship needs more freeboard, so the winter line is lower than the summer line. The Winter North Atlantic line is the lowest of all. In the tropics, the weather is often better. For this reason, the tropical line is higher than the summer line.

In fresh water, a ship floats deeper than in sea water. Fresh water is less dense than sea water. For this reason, the fresh water lines F and TF are higher. A ship in a river port can load a little deeper. When it reaches the sea, it rises again.

The oceans of the world are divided into areas. Each {{seasonal zone}} has its own rules for the load lines. In some zones, the line changes with the season of the year. The officer checks the zone chart for the whole voyage. The ship must respect the correct line in every zone.

{{Displacement}} is the total weight of the ship. It equals the weight of the water the hull pushes away. When the ship loads cargo, its displacement increases. The draft increases too, and the freeboard decreases. The officer reads the draft carefully and compares it with the correct mark.
