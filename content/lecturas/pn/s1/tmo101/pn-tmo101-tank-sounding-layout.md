A ship carries many liquids in tanks. Some liquids are for the voyage, like fuel and fresh water. Others are for stability and trim, like sea water ballast. The officers must know where each tank is and how much liquid it contains.

Many tanks are in the {{double bottom}}. This is the space between the outer bottom and the inner bottom of the hull. At the ends of the ship, there are the {{fore peak tank}} and the after peak tank. Some ships also have wing tanks along the sides. A {{ballast tank}} holds sea water. The crew fills or empties ballast tanks to change the draft, the trim and the stability. A {{fresh water tank}} holds water for drinking, washing and the engines. A {{fuel oil tank}} holds fuel for the main engine and the generators. Fuel tanks are usually near the engine room. Fresh water tanks must stay separate from tanks with oil or sea water.

How does the crew know the level of liquid in a tank? They take a sounding. Each tank has a {{sounding pipe}}. It goes from the deck down to the bottom of the tank. The crew puts a {{sounding tape}} with a small weight into the pipe. When the weight touches the bottom, the crew pulls the tape up. The wet part of the tape shows the depth of the liquid. Under the pipe, at the bottom of the tank, there is a {{striking plate}}. It protects the bottom plating from the weight. The pipe has a cap on top. The crew must close the cap after each sounding. Many ships also have remote gauges, but the sounding pipe is still important.

Every tank also needs an {{air pipe}}. When liquid goes into a tank, air must go out. When liquid goes out, air must come in. Without an air pipe, a tank can burst or collapse. The top of the air pipe is on the open deck. It has a special head with a closing device, often a float. The float closes the pipe when sea water comes on deck. This keeps the sea out of the tank.

Two other systems are also part of a familiarization round. The {{bilge}} is the lowest part inside the hull, where water collects. Bilge pumps remove this water. The {{fire main}} carries sea water under pressure to hydrants on deck. The crew connects fire hoses to these hydrants. Your teacher can give you more information about these two systems.
