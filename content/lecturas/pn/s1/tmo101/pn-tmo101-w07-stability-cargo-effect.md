{{Stability}} is the ability of a ship to return to the upright position. Wind and waves push the ship to one side all the time. A stable ship comes back after each push. Officers check the stability before departure and during cargo work.

{{Displacement}} is the weight of the ship. It includes the ship itself and everything on board. This means cargo, fuel, water, ballast, stores and people. A floating ship pushes away some water. The weight of this water is equal to the displacement.

All the weight of the ship acts at one point. This point is the {{centre of gravity}}, or G. The weight pulls down through G. The water pushes up through another point. This point is the {{centre of buoyancy}}, or B. B is at the centre of the underwater part of the hull. When the ship is upright, G and B are on the same vertical line.

Sometimes a wave makes the ship {{heel}}. Then the underwater shape of the hull changes. B moves to the lower side. Now the force up through B and the weight down through G are not on the same line. Together, they turn the ship. For small angles, the vertical line through the new B crosses the centre line at one point. This point is the {{metacentre}}, or M. The distance between G and M is the {{metacentric height}}, or GM. When M is above G, GM is positive. Then the ship returns to upright. When G is above M, GM is negative, and the ship is unstable. A ship with a small GM rolls slowly. A ship with a large GM rolls quickly and hard.

Cargo changes the position of G. Every weight on board has an effect on G. A weight added low in the ship moves G down. A weight added high in the ship moves G up. The officer must know how G moves, because GM depends on it.

Tanks also have an effect on stability. The ship uses {{ballast}} in its tanks to adjust the draft, the trim and the stability. A completely full tank or an empty tank is not a problem here. But a {{slack tank}} is a tank that is only partly full. When the ship heels, the liquid in it moves to the lower side. This moving liquid acts like a weight that moves G up. This is the {{free surface effect}}, and it reduces GM. Several slack tanks together can reduce stability a lot.

In your task, think about where the cargo is and which tanks are slack. Then explain how they change G and GM.
