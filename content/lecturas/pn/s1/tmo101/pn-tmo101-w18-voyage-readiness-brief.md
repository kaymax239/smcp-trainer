At the end of the semester, you bring all the topics of this course together. A {{voyage}} needs careful preparation. Before departure, the master wants a short report about the condition of the ship. This report is a {{readiness}} brief. The officer gives it in clear and simple English.

The brief starts with the ship. The officer describes the type of ship and its main parts. The {{hull}}, the bow and the stern must be in good condition. The anchoring and mooring equipment must be ready. The steering and propulsion systems must work. The crew tests them before departure.

Next, the officer reports the cargo and the tanks. The cargo is stowed, secured and lashed. Each {{hatch cover}} is closed and watertight. The officer knows the quantity in each ballast, fresh water and fuel tank. The soundings are recorded.

Then the officer reports the condition of the ship in the water. The {{draft}} is read on the draft marks forward, midships and aft. The officer states the {{trim}}. The {{freeboard}} must be enough for the voyage. The ship must not be deeper than the correct {{load line}} for the seasonal zone. The officer also reports the {{stability}}. The ship must have good stability for the whole voyage.

The hull stress is also part of the brief. The weight must be well distributed along the ship. This avoids {{hogging}} and {{sagging}}. If the weather forecast is bad, the officer explains the heavy-weather preparation. This includes the closures, the lashings and the safety of the crew.

Finally, the officer reports compliance. The certificates are on board and valid. The ship follows the IMO conventions. The crew has the right training and certificates for their jobs.

A good brief has a clear order. It goes from the ship, to the cargo, to the condition in the water, to the rules. Each point is short. The officer uses numbers from the ship's real records, not guesses. If something is not ready, the officer says it clearly.

The maritime sector changes all the time. Ships use new technology, new fuels and new digital systems. New rules also appear. For this reason, this course has a unit for current topics. Your teacher can choose these topics and connect them to the course. A good officer keeps learning during the whole career.
