Before a ship goes into {{heavy weather}}, the crew prepares the ship and the cargo. A good preparation protects the hull, the cargo and the people on board. The preparation uses three ideas from this course. They are hull stress, watertight closures and cargo securing.

First, think about hull stress. Waves do not support the hull equally. When a wave crest is at midships, the ends lose support. The middle of the ship bends up. This is {{hogging}}. When there are crests at the bow and the stern, the middle loses support. The middle of the ship bends down. This is {{sagging}}. In heavy seas, the hull changes between these two conditions again and again.

The bow receives other loads too. The water pressure on the bow plates changes with each wave. This small in-and-out movement of the plates is {{panting}}. Sometimes the bow comes out of the water and falls back hard. This is {{pounding}}. The bow and the stern have extra reinforcement for these loads. Still, the structure has limits.

Second, check the closures. Sea water must not enter the holds. Each hold has a {{hatch cover}}. The cover must be {{watertight}}. It closes on a rubber {{gasket}}. Before closing, the crew cleans the coamings and the drain channels. Dirt or cargo residue on the gasket can let water in. The crew also checks the cleats that hold the cover down. Other accesses to the holds must also be closed. Every {{watertight door}} on deck must be shut and secured. Ventilators and other openings need attention too.

Third, check the cargo. In a heavy roll, cargo can move. A moving cargo is very dangerous for the ship. The crew checks every {{lashing}} on deck and in the holds. Loose lashings must be tightened. Cargo on deck needs special attention, because the sea can hit it directly. The crew also secures loose gear, stores and equipment on deck.

The crew also thinks about safety on deck. In heavy weather, people must not go on the open deck without a reason. Before the bad weather comes, the work on deck should be finished.

Finally, the officer reports the preparation to the master. The report is short and clear. It covers the hull, the closures and the cargo. The deck watch can also do something to reduce the loads on the ship. In the task, you choose this action and explain the reason.
