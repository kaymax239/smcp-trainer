People used the sea for transport for thousands of years. In ancient times, sailors used small boats with oars and sails. They usually stayed close to the coast. They followed the coast, the sun and the stars. The Phoenicians, the Greeks and the Romans were great traders in the Mediterranean Sea. Their ships carried wine, oil, grain and metals between ports. This was the beginning of maritime trade.

Over many centuries, {{navigation}} improved. The magnetic {{compass}} gave sailors a direction at sea, even on cloudy nights. Bigger and stronger ships under {{sail}} crossed the oceans. Long voyages connected Europe, Africa, America and Asia. Ports grew, and trade grew with them.

In modern history, engines replaced sails. Steel ships became larger and safer. Today, a {{merchant ship}} can carry a very large amount of cargo. Modern navigation uses radar, satellite positions and electronic charts. But the basic idea is still the same. The ship must move cargo safely from one port to another. Most international trade moves by sea.

A basic maritime transport operation has several stages. First, the {{shipper}} gives the cargo to the carrier. The carrier accepts the cargo and issues a {{bill of lading}}. This document describes the cargo and works as a receipt. Next comes the {{loading}}. Port workers and the crew put the cargo on board and stow it. Then the ship sails to the port of destination. During the voyage, the watch officers check the ship and the cargo. At the destination, the ship completes the {{discharge}}. Finally, the {{consignee}} receives the cargo.

Maritime transport is often part of a longer chain. For example, a {{container}} can travel by truck to the port. Then it crosses the sea on a ship. After that, it can go by train to another city. This is {{multimodal transport}}. The ship is one link in the chain. The port connects the ship with land transport. Each link must work well. A delay at one link causes delays in the whole chain.

Modern deck work still uses old ideas. Sailors in the past watched the sea, the weather and the cargo all the time. Today, the deck watch does the same job with modern equipment. Think about the history of navigation and the stages of a cargo operation. You need both ideas for your brief.
